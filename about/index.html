ABOUT / BACKGROUND

About me.

I'm Jessica Todd, a roboticist and aerospace engineer working at the intersection of engineering systems and scientific exploration.

My work focuses on developing adaptive and intelligent robotic systems that can explore and gather scientific information in environments that are difficult for humans to access, like the surface of Mars or the depths of the ocean. My current research focuses on risk- and science-aware robotic planning for planetary robotics. Previously I've developed adaptive decision making and planning algorithms for scientific robots exploring coral reefs.

My research lies at the intersection of science modelling, decision-making under uncertainty, perception, and path planning. Fieldwork is an important part of how I approach robotics. From coral reefs to the Arctic to the Antarctic, I enjoy the challenges of deploying technology outside controlled laboratory settings and collaborating with scientists in the field, to return crucial scientific data.

I'm also passionate about developing scientific technology beyond robotics that can aid both Earth and planetary scientists in expedtionary science. I've worked on virtual and augmented reality interfaces for astronaut training and robotic control, self assembling observation towers for exploring craters on the lunar surface, and mission concept designs for scientific missions to Titan and Ceres. I've tested robots on the International Space Station, landed a camera on the Moon, and spen two weeks as a simulation Mars astronaut in the deserts of Utah.

I am a 2022 National Geographic Explorer for my work on coral reefs in the U.S. Virgin Islands, and current National Geographic Meridian Grant Recipient to support work investigating hydrothermal vents in Iceland.

When I'm not conducting fieldwork, I'm enjoying nature as a passionate SCUBA diver, or volunteering as an EMT.
